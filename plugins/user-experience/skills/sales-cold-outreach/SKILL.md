# Sales Cold Outreach

A cold email has one job: earn a reply. Everything after it — collateral, objections, the demo, the close — is a conversation the reply started, and each stage has its own small job too. Outreach fails when a stage tries to do the next stage's work: the email asks for a meeting, the demo tours every feature, the rep sells before discovering what hurts.

## When to use

- Drafting a cold email or a multi-touch sequence
- Producing a one-pager, pitch deck, comparison page, or battle card
- Preparing for or reviewing objection handling
- Planning a discovery call, a demo, or a trial-to-close motion
- Defining what marketing hands to sales and how fast sales must respond

## Protocol

1. **Structure the email to earn a reply.** Observation (specific to them) → problem (tied to that observation) → proof (a case or a number) → ask (interest-based and low friction: "worth a look?"). Test the personalisation: delete the first line — if the email still reads fine, the line was decoration.
2. **Sequence 3–5 touches, each with a new angle.** Every follow-up adds a resource, a result, or a different framing of the problem. Never send "just checking in"; a touch with nothing new teaches the prospect to ignore you.
3. **Arm the champion.** Someone inside must resell you when you are not in the room. Give them a one-pager readable in 30 seconds (problem, solution, three differentiators), a 10–12 slide deck built around the market shift and the return, and honest comparison pages that concede where a rival fits better.
4. **Handle objections with evidence, in order.** Acknowledge → clarify what is really being asked → respond with proof → confirm it landed. Pre-build battle cards for the three that always come: price (a return calculation), timing (a quick-win scenario), competition (a differentiator card).
5. **Discover before you demo.** Do not show a feature until you know the pain it relieves for this prospect. After each major feature ask, "does this solve the problem you described?" Gate trials on demonstrated value, not calendar time.
6. **Define the handoff as an SLA.** A marketing-qualified lead is fit (matches the persona) plus engagement (took a high-intent action). High-intent leads get a human response in minutes, not hours: lead-response studies show qualification odds collapse after the first hour, and same-day is the norm you are trying to beat.

## Red flags (rationalizations to reject)

- "The ask should be a meeting; that is what we want." — A calendar request is the highest-friction call to action in a cold email. Ask whether it is worth a look; the meeting comes after the reply.
- "A quick check-in keeps us top of mind." — Nothing new means no reason to reply, and the sequence is now noise.
- "Show them everything; something will stick." — A demo without discovery is a product tour, and the prospect cannot tell which part was for them.
- "Sales will get to the lead today." — Same-day is too slow for high intent; the next vendor answered within the hour.
