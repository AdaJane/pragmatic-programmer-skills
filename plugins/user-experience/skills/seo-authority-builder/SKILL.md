# SEO Authority Builder

Two pages can say the same true thing and only one will rank: the one that shows who is saying it, why they know, who else vouches for them, and how a reader could check. Authority is demonstrated, not declared: inventory what the site already shows for each of the four dimensions, find the gaps, and close them with artefacts a reader can check — author pages, editorial policy, citations, topical depth, entity markup.

## When to use

- Content is competitive on relevance but losing on trust
- The topic is YMYL (your money or your life): health, finance, legal, safety
- Reviewing author bios, about and team pages, editorial or correction policies
- Building topical authority around a subject cluster
- Deciding which schema types to add for entity clarity

## Signal inventory

| Dimension | Look for |
|---|---|
| Experience | First-hand accounts, case studies, original data, behind-the-scenes process |
| Expertise | Named authors with credentials, technical depth, accurate terminology, expert quotes |
| Authoritativeness | Citations to and from authoritative sources, brand mentions, recognition, published research |
| Trustworthiness | Contact details, privacy and terms, HTTPS, reviews, editorial and correction policies |

## Protocol

1. **Inventory before prescribing.** Walk the content and the site scaffolding against the table above and record what is present, weak, or absent per dimension.
2. **Close the credibility gaps.** Author bios with real credentials and an author page per contributor; an about page that says who runs the site and why; team, editorial-policy, fact-checking, and corrections pages; a reachable contact route.
3. **Build topical authority, not isolated posts.** Map the subject as a cluster, cover it to depth, interlink the cluster deliberately, use the vocabulary of the field, and connect the entities (people, organisations, concepts) the topic rests on.
4. **Show the receipts.** Cite primary sources; link out to authoritative references; surface reviews, testimonials, certifications, partnerships, and media mentions where they are true.
5. **Mark up the entities.** Person schema for authors, Organization for the publisher, Article for the content, each with `sameAs` links to the entity's profiles elsewhere, so engines can attach the trust signals to the right entity.
6. **Deliver a plan, not a lecture.** Per dimension: what is present, what is missing, and the artefact that closes the gap, in priority order.

## Red flags (rationalizations to reject)

- "Add a trust badge and a testimonials widget." — Decoration without a named author or a checkable claim signals the opposite of trust.
- "Our writer knows the field; the bio can say 'expert'." — Unverifiable adjectives are not credentials. Show the qualification, the work, or the affiliation.
- "E-E-A-T is a ranking factor, so optimise for it." — It is how quality is judged, not a dial. Fabricated credentials buy nothing an engine can't ignore and become a liability the moment a reader checks.
- "Cover the keywords and topical authority follows." — Coverage is breadth; authority is depth plus recognition.
